The story

Three runs. One quiet backend change.

1

Day 1, 3:00 PM — First run

PASS

You click Run. Contract Watch fetches the endpoint and compares the response against your schema. Everything matches.

{
  "user_id": 1,
  "name": "Souvik",
  "email": "souvik@example.com"
}

Diffs: none

2

Day 3, 11:00 AM — Still passing

PASS

Nothing changed on the backend. Same response, same comparison, same result. Your screen shows PASS.

Diffs: none

3

Day 7, 2:00 PM — Drift detected

DRIFT DETECTED

Your teammate renames user_id to userId and changes it from a number to a string. She forgets to tell you. You click Run.

{
  "userId": "1",
  "name": "Souvik",
  "email": "souvik@example.com"
}
  • Field renamed — user_id became userId
  • Type changed — integer became string

The API was working correctly until Day 7 at 2 PM. That is when the break happened. Now you know exactly where to look and exactly what to tell your teammate changed.

Acknowledge

Expected drift? Stop the noise.

Your teammate confirms the change was intentional — you're moving to camelCase everywhere. Update your schema, but first hit Acknowledge on that run.

We know about this

Acknowledge tells Contract Watch: stop flagging this as a new problem. It stays in history — it does not disappear — but the dashboard stops shouting about it.

Scheduler & alerts — the future flow

Version one needs you to click Run. The scheduler removes that person from the equation entirely.

Automated scheduler

Set it up once — check this contract every 15 minutes, every hour, or every day at 9 AM. Contract Watch keeps running in the background. Nobody has to open the app.

Run 2   Mon 12:15 AM   PASS

Slack & email alerts

Instead of opening the app to check, Contract Watch messages you the moment drift is detected — which contract broke and exactly what changed.

Scheduler detects drift → Saves run with diffs → Posts to Slack

Why not in v1?

  • A scheduler needs a server running 24/7 — v1 runs on localhost, so it stops when you close your laptop.
  • Building it with APScheduler is the perfect contributor task — self-contained, testable, and genuinely useful to merge.
